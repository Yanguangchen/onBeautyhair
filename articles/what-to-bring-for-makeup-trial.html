What to Bring for Makeup Trial Day

May 2, 2026 Updated Sep 28, 2026 5 min read

By the On Hair Salon & Beauty team

The short answer

Bring three to five unfiltered reference photos (plus one look you dislike), your outfit's colour and neckline, your hairstyle plan, and notes on skin sensitivities and products you love. Arrive with clean, moisturised skin, wear a top with a similar neckline to your outfit, and photograph the finished look in daylight, indoor light and with flash before you decide.

A makeup trial is a rehearsal, not a reveal. It's your chance to test a look, adjust it, and make sure the person in the mirror still feels like you, just on a very good day. The more your artist knows about the event, the outfit and your skin, the closer the first attempt lands.

Here's what to bring, what to ask and how to judge the result so the real day holds no surprises.

The Trial-Day Kit

  • Reference photos: three to five looks you love and one you don't.
  • Outfit details: a photo of the outfit, or at least its colour, neckline and fabric.
  • Hair plan: up, down or half-up, plus any accessories you'll wear.
  • Skin notes: allergies, sensitivities and products that irritate you.
  • Your favourite products: a lipstick or foundation shade you love is a useful starting point.
  • A similar top: wear something with a neckline close to your outfit's.
  • Your phone: for photos in different lighting before you leave.

References That Actually Help

Filters are the enemy of a good trial. A beauty filter can smooth skin, shift colours and even reshape features, so the look you're chasing may not exist in real life. Choose photos taken in natural light, ideally of people with a skin tone and eye shape similar to yours.

Be specific about what you love in each photo. Is it the glowy skin, the soft brown eyeliner or the blush placement? One picture rarely gets everything right, and pointing at the details helps your artist combine them.

Artist's tip: the "never" photo is gold. Knowing you hate heavy contour or glitter on the lids saves far more time than another photo of a look you like.

Outfit and Accessories

Makeup works in balance with everything around it. A strong red lip may compete with a bold patterned dress, while a soft pink might disappear against a bright gown. Share your outfit's colour and neckline, whether the fabric is matte or shiny, and any statement jewellery or hair accessories. For traditional or cultural outfits, mention the colours and any headpieces, since these change how much colour the face can carry.

Your Hair Plan

Hair and makeup are designed together. An updo shows more of your face and jawline, so blush and contour placement matter more. Hair worn down frames the face and can soften a stronger makeup look.

Time your hair appointments too. A colour refresh one to two weeks before the event gives the shade time to settle, and a trim three to seven days before keeps the shape fresh without looking just-cut. Our colour appointment guide has the full prep list.

Skin Notes and Sensitivities

Tell your artist about allergies, breakouts, dry patches, oily areas and anything that has irritated your skin before, including lash glue. If you're planning lash extensions or a lash lift for the event, mention it, as it changes how eye makeup is applied.

Arrive with clean, moisturised skin and no heavy makeup, so your artist sees your skin as it really is. If you're also planning a facial, our facial timing guide shows when to book it so your skin is calm on both days.

The Singapore Heat Test

Local reality check: a look that's flawless in an air-conditioned room can slide in thirty minutes outdoors. After your trial, step outside for a short walk, then check your T-zone, under-eyes and lip line. Ask your artist about long-wear, sweat-resistant products and a touch-up kit.

Then photograph the look in three kinds of light, because your guests and photographer will see all three:

LightingWhat to check
Daylight by a windowDoes the foundation match your neck and chest? Is anything patchy or too heavy?
Warm indoor lightDo the eyes and lips still read clearly, or do they fade?
Camera flashAny white cast from powder or sunscreen? Is there too much shine?

Questions to Ask Your Artist

  1. How long will this look last in heat and humidity?
  2. What should I carry for touch-ups during the event?
  3. What would you change if I wanted this softer, or stronger?
  4. How early should I be ready on the day, and how long will it take?
The perfect trial ends with you forgetting you're wearing makeup and remembering how good you felt in it.

After the Trial

Wear the look for a few hours if you can, and notice how it feels. Does anything crease, feel heavy or catch the light oddly? Write down what you'd change while it's fresh, and share your notes and photos with your artist so the final version is dialled in.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I schedule a makeup trial?

For weddings and big events, many people book their trial one to three months ahead. That leaves time for a second trial or a change of direction without last-minute stress.

Should I arrive with a bare face?

Yes, or as close to it as possible. Come with clean, moisturised skin so your artist can see your real skin tone and texture.

Can I bring my own products?

Bring any products you love or know your skin tolerates well. They help your artist understand your preferences, even if they use their own kit.

How long does a makeup trial take?

It varies with the look and how many adjustments you try, so set aside a relaxed block of time rather than squeezing it between errands.

Final Thoughts

A good trial is a conversation. Bring your references, outfit details and skin notes, test the look in real light and real heat, and don't be shy about asking for changes. That's what the trial is for.
