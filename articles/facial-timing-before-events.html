Facial Timing Before Events

May 8, 2026 Updated Sep 28, 2026 5 min read

By the On Hair Salon & Beauty team

The short answer

Book deeper facials with extractions or exfoliation two to four weeks before your event, a gentle hydrating facial about one week before, and nothing new in the final 72 hours. If you've never had a particular treatment, try it six to eight weeks ahead, so there's time to recover if your skin disagrees.

Wedding, graduation, milestone dinner or a long-awaited photoshoot: everyone wants skin that looks rested and quietly radiant on the day. The treatment matters, but the timing matters just as much. A brilliant facial booked two days too late can leave you with redness the camera will happily catch.

Think of your skin prep less like a single appointment and more like a countdown. Here's how to plan it.

Why Timing Matters

Many facials are active treatments. Deep cleansing, extractions and exfoliation work by clearing and renewing the skin, and that process can bring temporary redness, mild swelling or small breakouts as congestion comes to the surface. Skin usually settles within days, but everyone's timeline is different.

Your skin also renews itself gradually, roughly over a month for many adults. Starting early gives your skin time to go through that cycle, so the results show on the day and any reaction has long faded.

The Countdown

  1. Six to eight weeks before: experiment here, not later. Try any treatment you haven't had before, and settle into a simple, consistent skincare routine you'll keep until the event.
  2. Two to four weeks before: the deep work. This is the window for deep-cleansing facials and extractions. If your skin reacts, there's time for it to calm down.
  3. About one week before: soothe and hydrate. Switch to gentle, moisture-focused care. A calming Face Spa rather than an intensive treatment helps skin look plump and even.
  4. Two to three days before: hands off. No new products, no strong exfoliation, no experiments. Focus on water, sleep and your familiar routine.
  5. The night before: rest. A gentle cleanse, a good moisturiser and an early night do more than any last-minute mask.
  6. On the day: keep it light. Cleanse gently, moisturise, apply sunscreen, and tell your makeup artist about anything your skin is sensitive to.

Planning for Singapore Heat

The humidity factor: outdoor ceremonies, garden photoshoots and hot walks between venues all test your skin and makeup. Well-hydrated skin holds makeup better and looks less oily, so the week-before hydrating step matters here. Keep blotting papers handy, and ask your makeup artist about long-wear, sweat-resistant products.

Moving between hot outdoor air and cold air-conditioning also dehydrates skin quickly. Drinking enough water in the final week and using a lightweight moisturiser helps your skin stay balanced rather than tight.

The Final-Week Don'ts

  • Don't start new actives. Hold off on new retinoids, strong acids or anything you haven't used before.
  • Don't do at-home peels or extractions. Picking is the fastest route to a mark that lasts longer than a pimple.
  • Don't wax or thread for the first time. Stick to anything you've done before without a reaction.
  • Don't get sunburnt. Wear sunscreen daily and seek shade, especially before an outdoor event.
  • Don't switch foundation. Test any new makeup at your trial, not the night before.
The secret to event-day skin isn't one miracle treatment. It's a calm, consistent plan that starts earlier than you think.

If You Break Out Anyway

It happens to everyone. Resist the urge to squeeze. Keep the area clean, use a gentle spot treatment you've used before, and tell your makeup artist, who can colour-correct and conceal far more effectively than you'd expect. A calm, flat blemish is much easier to cover than an inflamed one you've picked at.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days before a wedding should I get a facial?

Book deep-cleansing facials with extractions two to four weeks before, and a gentle hydrating facial about a week before. Avoid anything new in the final three days.

Can I get a facial the day before an event?

Only a very gentle, familiar one, if any. Active treatments can cause temporary redness or breakouts, so the day before is best kept for rest, hydration and your usual routine.

Is it okay to have extractions one week before?

It's cutting it close. Extractions can leave skin red or tender for a few days, so two to four weeks before is safer. If you must, tell your therapist about the event date so they can adjust the treatment.

What should I do the night before my event?

Cleanse gently, moisturise, drink water and get an early night. Skip new masks, exfoliants and anything your skin hasn't tried before.

Summary Timeline

  • 6 to 8 weeks: try new treatments and settle your routine.
  • 2 to 4 weeks: deep cleansing and extractions.
  • 1 week: hydrating, soothing care.
  • Final 72 hours: nothing new, gentle and familiar only.
